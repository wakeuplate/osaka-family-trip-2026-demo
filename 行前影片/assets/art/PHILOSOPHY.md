# Natsu Refrain（夏のリフレイン）

A movement of summer restraint: the visual language of a Japanese festival seen from a paper window. It borrows the discipline of Edo pattern-makers — who repeated one curve ten thousand times until repetition itself became devotion — and the warmth of a family album. Every artifact must look meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention, as if a master printmaker labored over each arc with a worn bamboo baren.

**Space and form.** Forms are flat, cut with the confidence of katazome stencils. No gradients pretending to be light; instead, shapes so precisely balanced they feel inevitable. Negative space is the host of the composition — motifs occupy little, breathe much. A daruma is not drawn; it is distilled to five shapes that could not be four.

**Color and material.** A strict palette: warm washi cream (#FAF5EC), vermilion (#D7402B), deep indigo (#22406B), and a whisper of gold (#C9A227). Color is applied like layered ink — solid, matte, slightly asymmetric in spirit even when geometrically exact. The paper is always allowed to show through; white is never white, it is paper.

**Scale and rhythm.** Repetition is the heartbeat: seigaiha waves repeat like breath, rays repeat like festival drums. Rhythm is regular but never mechanical — the eye should sense a human hand counting the intervals. Large singular motifs (one daruma, one torii) are soloists against these choral patterns.

**Composition and balance.** Asymmetry resolved by weight, not by centering. Every element sits within generous margins; nothing touches an edge uninvited, nothing overlaps carelessly. Hierarchy is carried by scale and pigment density alone. Text, if it exists at all, is a seal — two or three characters at most, placed like a signature stamp, never a caption.

The result must read as an heirloom: analytical in its geometry, tender in its intent, and unmistakably the work of countless patient hours at the absolute top of the craft.
